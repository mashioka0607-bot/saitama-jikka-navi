# SERP / monetization check — 2026-09-27 23:04 JST

## Decision change

The previous differentiation claim — “municipal subsidy + cleanup/demolition cost + sale price in one proceeds calculator” — is no longer sufficiently unique by itself.

Akiya Compass (akiya-compass.jp) is now visible with a 7-question diagnostic that says it estimates whether an empty house can sell and how much remains, using public land prices and municipal subsidy data. House-closing competitors also continue to push “appraise before cleanup/demolition” directly from the SERP.

Therefore the site should not compete by adding thin municipality pages or by claiming the calculator itself is unique. The next product differentiation should be **decision accuracy after real quotes arrive**: compare actual brokerage/buyout quotes on a common net-proceeds basis, including who bears residual-property disposal, cleanup, demolition, holding cost and confirmed subsidy. This is closer to conversion and harder to replace with a generic estimated-price calculator.

## Monetization

Town Life Akiya Solution remains a strong fit: official 2026 releases say it can introduce providers in all 47 prefectures / 1,718 municipalities and had 650+ partner providers by April 2026. The public web still does not provide a trustworthy account-specific affiliate tracking URL, so do not insert a normal service URL as if it were an affiliate link.

A third-party affiliate-industry page currently reports a LIXIL Real Estate Shop vacant-house purchase offer via closed ASP felmat at 30,800 yen per result. Treat this as a **lead to verify inside the ASP**, not as a confirmed live payout or approved offer for this site.

## Primary-source / quality rule

Municipal subsidy data remains useful but volatile. Keep it in the maintained checker, cite municipality/prefecture primary sources, and avoid municipality-template expansion. The calculator must label subsidy as confirmed/estimated and never imply eligibility or award.
